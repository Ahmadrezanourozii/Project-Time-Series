\begin{abstract}
Published accuracies for Parkinson's disease detection from gait force
recordings span 70--99\,\% on the PhysioNet database, a spread wider than the
differences between the models involved. We classify patients against controls
with a Mamba state-space model on 100 subjects, under two evaluation protocols
on identical data. Held to subject-disjoint splits, with the configuration
chosen on validation rather than on test, the model reaches $75 \pm 4$\,\%
accuracy and $0.85$ AUC on both feet, slightly ahead of Random Forest,
SVM-RBF, 1D-ResNet and LSTM baselines. The gain comes from describing each
second by its channel statistics instead of feeding raw samples, which also
removes a training collapse seen on two folds. Re-running the same baselines
with window-wise splits, where windows of one subject fall on both sides,
lifts Random Forest accuracy from 73\,\% to 98\,\%. We report the
subject-wise numbers, and the window-wise ones only as a caution.
\end{abstract}
