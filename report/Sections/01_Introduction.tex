\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Parkinson's disease (PD) is the second most common neurodegenerative disorder,
and gait disturbance is among its earliest and most characteristic motor
signs. Clinical rating scales such as the UPDRS depend on expert observation
and are coarsely quantised, which motivates objective, sensor-based measures.
Instrumented insoles record the vertical ground reaction force (VGRF) under
each foot at high rate and low cost, and the resulting multivariate time
series carries the stride-timing irregularity, reduced swing time and
left--right asymmetry that characterise parkinsonian gait \cite{hausdorff2007}.

The methodological question this report addresses is not only \emph{which}
model classifies these signals best, but \emph{how a reported accuracy should
be read}. Published accuracies on the PhysioNet gait database span roughly
70--99\,\%, a spread far larger than the differences between the model
families involved. A large part of that spread comes from how recordings are
split: when a recording is cut into windows and those windows are then divided
at random, windows of the same subject appear in both training and test data.
A classifier can then recognise the individual rather than the disease. Our
experiments make that effect explicit and quantify it on identical data.

\subsection{Related work}
\label{ssec:sota}
Classical pipelines on this database combine hand-crafted statistics of the
VGRF channels with Random Forests or kernel SVMs. Deep models -- 1D-ResNets,
recurrent networks and, more recently, transformers -- operate on the raw
signal or on sequences of feature vectors. Structured state-space models
(S4, and subsequently Mamba \cite{gu2023mamba}) achieve transformer-level
sequence modelling at linear cost in sequence length by making the state
transition input-dependent (selective scan), which makes them attractive for
long physiological recordings. Mamba has been applied to speech, EEG and ECG,
but to our knowledge not yet to VGRF-based PD classification under a
subject-disjoint protocol.

\subsection{Contributions of this work}
\label{ssec:contr}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A Mamba classifier for VGRF gait signals that outperforms the Random
        Forest, SVM-RBF, 1D-ResNet and LSTM baselines under a subject-disjoint
        protocol, together with an ablation of encoder size and of the
        bidirectional extension of the otherwise causal state-space scan.
  \item A token design study. Feeding the raw 100\,Hz signal makes training
        collapse on two of five folds; representing each second by its
        channel statistics removes the collapse and raises subject-level
        accuracy from 69\,\% to 76\,\% at matched settings.
  \item A quantified comparison of the subject-wise and window-wise
        evaluation protocols on the same data, model and metrics, showing an
        inflation of about twenty accuracy points from leakage alone.
\end{itemize}
