\subsection{Data}
\label{ssec:data}
We use the PhysioNet \emph{Gait in Parkinson's Disease} database
\cite{goldberger2000physiobank}, pooling its three studies (Ga, Ju, Si). Each
subject walked on level ground for about two minutes while eight force sensors
per foot sampled at 100\,Hz; the dataset provides the 16 sensor channels plus
the total force under each foot. Our subset comprises the 100 subjects with a
normal-walk recording, balanced by design into 50 PD patients and 50 healthy
controls (HC). Table~\ref{tab:cohort} summarizes it: the groups are matched on
sex, age and body size and separate on exactly the variables the disease is
expected to move, gait speed and the timed up-and-go test. Heights are given in
centimeters after harmonizing the two unit conventions of the source file,
which records 65 entries in meters and 32 in centimeters.

The course-provided five-fold partition assigns 60 training, 20 validation and
20 test subjects per fold, class-balanced, with test sets disjoint across folds
so that pooling them covers every subject exactly once. The folds are not
stratified by sub-study, whose PD rates differ (0.58, 0.50 and 0.43 for Ga, Ju
and Si), a confound we return to in Section~\ref{sec:disc}.

\subsubsection{Pre-processing}
The first five seconds of each recording are discarded to remove gait
initiation. Each window inherits its subject's label, and channel normalization
is a per-channel z-score whose mean and standard deviation are computed over
the training windows of the current fold only, then applied unchanged to
validation and test.

Three input representations were compared, each with its own segmentation.
\textbf{(i) Raw samples}: the recording is cut into 5\,s windows with a 1\,s
step, giving a $18 \times 500$ matrix per window and 10\,407 windows in total,
between 35 and 254 per subject. \textbf{(ii) Amplitude-statistic features}: each
second of signal becomes one feature vector holding the 11 statistics of
Table~\ref{tab:stats} for each of the 18 channels, i.e.\ 198 dimensions; these
vectors are then grouped into windows of 30 or 60 seconds with a step of 10 or 15,
which yields 827 and 363 windows respectively, between 1 and 14 per subject at
60\,s. \textbf{(iii) Stride-cycle features}: one feature vector per gait cycle carrying
stride, stance and swing time, stance ratio, normalized peak force and impulse,
stride-time increment and double-support time for both feet; contacts are
detected by thresholding each foot's total force at 5\,\% of its maximum, and
strides outside 0.5--2.5\,s are discarded. The reported model uses
representation (ii) with 60\,s windows.

\subsubsection{Materials/Instruments used}
Models were trained on a Kaggle NVIDIA Tesla T4 (16\,GB, compute capability
7.5) with PyTorch 2.10 (CUDA 12.8) and \texttt{mamba-ssm} 2.3.2, using mixed
precision. Development, feature extraction and evaluation ran locally on a
machine with an Apple M1 chip with 8GB RAM. Code was version-controlled on
GitHub and pulled by the Kaggle notebook at the start of every run, so each
reported number comes from a known commit.

\subsection{Proposed approach}
\label{ssec:mod}

\subsubsection{Mamba classifier}
A Mamba block is a selective state-space layer: its state transition and input
projections are functions of the input, so the recurrence can forget or retain
information conditioned on content, and it runs in linear time in the sequence
length \cite{gu2023mamba}. Standard Mamba is causal, which suits language
modeling but discards the right-hand context when an entire window is available
at inference. We therefore also implemented a bidirectional block (a forward
mixer and, after reversal, a second mixer with independent parameters, summed
before the residual connection) and ablate it in Section~\ref{ssec:arch}. The
reported model uses it.

The classifier projects each feature vector to $d_{\text{model}}$, applies $L$ such blocks,
and pools over time by concatenating the mean and the maximum of the final
representation, which a linear layer maps to two logits. Three sizes were
studied ($d_{\text{model}}, L$) $\in \{(64,2),$\allowbreak\ $(128,4),$\allowbreak\ $(256,6)\}$; the
configuration selected in Section~\ref{ssec:selection} uses the middle one.

\subsubsection{Training strategy}
Per fold we grid-search dropout $\in \{0.1, 0.3\}$ and learning rate
$\in \{3\!\cdot\!10^{-4}, 10^{-4}\}$, training with AdamW (weight decay
$10^{-3}$), three warm-up epochs followed by cosine decay, batch size 128 and
at most 30 epochs, with early stopping on validation ROC-AUC (patience 6,
minimum 8 epochs). The configuration with the highest validation ROC-AUC is
retrained on the union of training and validation subjects for the epoch count
optimal on validation, and that model predicts the test subjects, the
deep-learning equivalent of \texttt{GridSearchCV(refit=True)} on a predefined
split. Because some folds occasionally converged to a degenerate solution, a
fold whose best validation AUC stays below 0.70 is retrained with a different
seed (at most three attempts). The final model averages three seeds.

\subsubsection{Decision rule and subject-level aggregation}
The network scores windows, but the clinical unit is the subject, so a
subject's score is the mean of its window scores. The decision threshold is
calibrated per fold on that fold's \emph{validation} subjects by maximizing
balanced accuracy, using the model trained on training data only; test data
never influences it. The fixed threshold 0.5 differs by at most two points.

\subsubsection{Deployment on unseen cohorts}
Each fold's weights are exported with its threshold and the per-channel
statistics of \emph{that fold's training subjects}. A new recording is
normalized with those stored statistics (never with statistics recomputed
from the new cohort, which would leak its distribution into its own predictions)
and the five folds' scores are averaged.

\subsubsection{Evaluation metrics}
We report accuracy, precision, recall and F1 (weighted by class support, so
recall equals accuracy by construction), with sensitivity, specificity, ROC-AUC
and confusion matrices. All values are the mean and standard deviation over
1000 bootstrap resamples that draw whole subjects, so windows of one subject
are never counted as independent evidence. Each accuracy is tested against the
chance level with a one-sided exact binomial test over the 100 subject
decisions, and differences between models, which are evaluated on the same
subjects, with DeLong's test for AUC and an exact McNemar test for accuracy.
