\section{Conclusion}

We classified Parkinson's disease from vertical ground reaction force
recordings with a Mamba state-space model and evaluated it under a strictly
subject-disjoint protocol. The model attains $78 \pm 4$\,\% accuracy, $0.78$
weighted F1 and $0.82$ AUC on both feet, and $79 \pm 4$\,\% on the right foot,
outperforming Random Forest, SVM-RBF, 1D-ResNet and LSTM baselines evaluated
identically. The gain came from the input representation -- describing each
second by its channel statistics instead of feeding raw samples, which also
removed a training collapse on two folds -- and from encoder capacity, while
the bidirectional extension of the causal scan brought no measurable benefit
at matched size.

The second contribution is methodological. Re-running the same models with a
window-wise split -- the split implicitly used in much of the literature on
this database -- raises accuracy above 95\,\% for the Random Forest and to
88\,\% for the SVM, without any change to features, models or metrics. On a
dataset where a single recording yields more than a hundred correlated
windows, the split unit is not a detail; a published accuracy on this database
is uninterpretable unless the split unit is stated. We therefore report the
subject-wise numbers as our result and the window-wise numbers only as a
reference for what the same pipeline would claim under the weaker protocol.

\textbf{Future directions.} The most promising direction is data: pooling the
dual-task recordings and other public VGRF cohorts would enlarge an effective
sample of 100 subjects that currently limits every model here. Methodologically,
richer gait-cycle descriptors (long-range stride-time correlations, asymmetry
indices) fused with the amplitude tokens are worth pursuing, since the two
families were complementary in our subject-level analysis. Finally, calibrated
probabilities and per-subject uncertainty would matter more than raw accuracy
for a screening tool, and severity regression against UPDRS is a natural
extension of the same architecture.
