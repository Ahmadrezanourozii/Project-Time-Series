\section{Conclusion}

We classified Parkinson's disease from vertical ground reaction force recordings
with a Mamba state-space model, evaluated on five predefined subject-disjoint
folds. On both feet it reaches $75 \pm 4$\,\% accuracy, $0.75$ weighted F1 and
$0.85$ AUC at subject level, marginally ahead of the Random Forest, SVM-RBF,
1D-ResNet and LSTM baselines run through the same evaluation code, though the
Random Forest remains better on the right foot alone. What produced the result
was the input representation rather than the architecture: the bidirectional
extension of the causal scan, which we expected to help, made no measurable
difference at matched size.

The methodological result may be the more useful one. Re-running the same
baselines with a window-wise split raises Random Forest accuracy from 73\,\% to
98\,\% and SVM accuracy from 68\,\% to 88\,\%, without touching the features,
the models or the metrics. When one recording produces more than a hundred
correlated windows, the split unit is not an implementation detail, and an
accuracy quoted for this database cannot be interpreted unless the split unit is
stated alongside it. For the same reason we selected our final configuration on
validation AUC rather than on test accuracy, which costs about three points but
makes the reported number an estimate of performance on new subjects rather than
a summary of the search.

What we would do next is get more data before doing anything else. Every model
we tried, classical or deep, plateaued in the same narrow band, and the
subject-level analysis suggests the ceiling belongs to the cohort rather than to
the method: 100 subjects means one person is worth one accuracy point. Pooling
the dual-task recordings and other public VGRF cohorts is the obvious first
step. After that, the stride-cycle descriptors are worth revisiting, since they
proved complementary to the amplitude statistics at subject level even though
they were weak on their own. Finally, a screening tool needs calibrated
probabilities and some notion of per-subject confidence more than it needs
another point of accuracy, and the same architecture would extend naturally to
regressing UPDRS severity instead of a binary label.
